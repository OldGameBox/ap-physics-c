## (a) Experimental Procedure for Cart 2 Mass
Measure the total length of the horizontal track $L$ with your meterstick. Place Cart 1 and Cart 2 together with the compressed spring. Position the cart pair a distance $d_1$ from the left wall. Calculate the distance to the right wall as $d_2 = L - d_1$. Release the spring to launch the carts. Observe the carts to see which cart strikes the wall first. Adjust the starting position $d_1$ until both carts strike their walls at the same time. Record the distances $d_1$ and $d_2$ for this simultaneous strike. Add a block of known mass $m_{block}$ to Cart 1. Repeat the adjustment process for five different total masses of Cart 1. Record the total mass $m_{total1} = m_{cart1} + m_{blocks}$ for each trial. Perform three trials for each mass to average the results and reduce uncertainty.
#### (b) Linear Graphing and Analysis
Calculate the ratio $d_2 / d_1$ for every data point. Graph $m_{total1}$ on the vertical axis. Graph the ratio $d_2 / d_1$ on the horizontal axis. Conservation of momentum for carts starting from rest follows $0 = m_{total1} v_1 - M v_2$. The carts travel for the same time $t$ to hit the walls. The velocity relation $v = d / t$ implies $m_{total1} (d_1 / t) = M (d_2 / t)$. The equation simplifies to $m_{total1} = M (d_2 / d_1)$. The graph of $m_{total1}$ versus $d_2 / d_1$ produces a straight line. The slope of the line equals the unknown mass $M$ of Cart 2.
## (c) Inelastic Collision Analysis
#### (i) Graphed Quantities and Data
Horizontal axis: $v_{1i}^2 \text{ in } \text{m}^2 / \text{s}^2$
Vertical axis: $v_{1f}^2 + v_{3f}^2 \text{ in } \text{m}^2 / \text{s}^2$

The energy ratio $\beta = K_f / K_i$ becomes $\beta = (\frac{1}{2} m_1 v_{1f}^2 + \frac{1}{2} m_1 v_{3f}^2) / (\frac{1}{2} m_1 v_{1i}^2)$. Mass and the factor of one half cancel. The equation is $(v_{1f}^2 + v_{3f}^2) = \beta (v_{1i}^2)$.

Trial 1: $v_{1i}^2 = 4.00$, $v_{1f}^2 + v_{3f}^2 = 3.04$
Trial 2: $v_{1i}^2 = 16.00$, $v_{1f}^2 + v_{3f}^2 = 12.42$
Trial 3: $v_{1i}^2 = 36.00$, $v_{1f}^2 + v_{3f}^2 = 25.45$
Trial 4: $v_{1i}^2 = 64.00$, $v_{1f}^2 + v_{3f}^2 = 43.33$
Trial 5: $v_{1i}^2 = 100.00$, $v_{1f}^2 + v_{3f}^2 = 64.78$

#### (d) Experimental Value Calculation
Determine the slope of the best-fit line to find the value of $\beta$. Choose the point $(100, 64.78)$ from the data and the origin $(0, 0)$.
$\beta = \frac{64.78 - 0}{100 - 0}$
$\beta = 0.6478$
The experimental value for the energy ratio is $0.65$.
