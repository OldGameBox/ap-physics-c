# Rolling Ball Lab Report

## Objective

The objective is to analyze the kinetic energy of a ball rolling down a ramp. We compare the theoretical velocity against the experimental velocity. The model incorporates translational and rotational kinetic energy.

## Mathematical Model

Conservation of energy determines the final velocity. Initial potential energy transforms into translational and rotational kinetic energy.

$$mgh=\frac{1}{2}mv^2+\frac{1}{2}I\omega^2$$

For a solid rolling sphere:

$$I=\frac{2}{5}mr^2$$

$$\omega=\frac{v}{r}$$

Substitute the moment of inertia and angular velocity into the energy equation:

$$mgh=\frac{1}{2}mv^2+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{2}{5}mr^2\right)\left(\frac{v}{r}\right)^2$$

$$mgh=\frac{1}{2}mv^2+\frac{1}{5}mv^2$$

$$mgh=\frac{7}{10}mv^2$$

Solve for velocity:

$$v=\sqrt{\frac{10}{7}gh}$$

## Apparatus and Measurement Points

The setup includes a physics stand and a ramp. We use photogate timers to measure velocity at three locations.

- Point A is about half way to the first trough.
    
- Point B is the trough.
    
- Point C is the top of the hill after the trough.
    

## Experimental Data

The ball has a diameter of 1.5 cm and a radius of 0.0075 m. The base height measures 41.5 cm.

### Point A

- Change in height: 15.2 cm
    
- Average time: 0.0101 s
    
- Theoretical velocity: 1.46 m/s
    
- Experimental velocity: 1.49 m/s
    
- Percent error: 2.05%
    

### Point B

- Change in height: 22.9 cm
    
- Average time: 0.0092 s
    
- Theoretical velocity: 1.79 m/s
    
- Experimental velocity: 1.63 m/s
    
- Percent error: 8.94%
    

### Point C

- Change in height: 7.3 cm
    
- Average time: 0.0187 s
    
- Theoretical velocity: 1.01 m/s
    
- Experimental velocity: 0.8 m/s
    
- Percent error: 20.79%
    

## Error Analysis

The experimental velocities deviate from the theoretical predictions. Point A yields a 2.05% error. Point B yields an 8.94% error. Point C yields a 20.79% error. The percent error increases as the ball travels further along the track. Our formula did not include friction. Friction removes energy from the system over time. The energy lost to friction explains the discrepancy between the calculated and measured velocities.
