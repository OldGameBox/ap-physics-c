# (a)
To verify the series spring constant equation, suspend a known mass from a single spring and measure its equilibrium displacement using a meter stick to determine its individual constant $k$. Connect two or more identical springs in a vertical chain, hang various known masses from the bottom, and record the total extension of the series system for each mass. Repeat the process with different combinations of springs and masses to ensure a robust dataset for calculating the experimental equivalent spring constant $k_{eq}$.
## (b) Data Analysis
Plot the hanging weight $F_g = mg$ on the vertical axis against the measured total extension $\Delta x$ on the horizontal axis for the series combination. The slope of the resulting linear best-fit line represents the experimental equivalent spring constant $k_{eq}$ for that specific configuration. Compare this experimental slope to the theoretical value calculated using the reciprocal sum formula $1/k_{eq} = \sum 1/k_n$ to check for consistency.

## (d) Calculation of $k_3$
Using the line shown in the plot, the value is determined by the change in the force ratio over the change in squared displacement.
$$k_3 = \text{slope} = \frac{10.64 - 10.15}{0.2673 - 0.0388}$$
$$k_3 \approx 2.14 \text{ N/m}^3$$
