## notes
- ### lesson 1
	- Motion in two dimensions can be analyzed using one-dimensional kinematic relationships if the motion is separated into components.
	- [[Velocity]] and [[acceleration]] may be different in each dimension and may be nonuniform.
	- Motion in one dimension may be changed without causing a change in motion in a perpendicular dimension.
	- Projectile motion is a special case of two-dimensional motion that has zero acceleration in one dimension and constant, nonzero acceleration in the second dimension.
- ### lesson 2
	- The motion of a projectile can be analyzed qualitatively by considering the horizontal and vertical components of initial [[velocity]] independently.
	- The vertical component of initial [[velocity]] determines the time the projectile is in the air as well as its maximum height.
	- The horizontal component of the initial velocity determines the speed at which it can cover horizontal distance.
	- The maximum range of a projectile launched at an able and returning to its starting height is $45^{\circ}$.
- ### lesson 3
	- Motion in two dimensions can be analyzed using one-dimensional kinematic relationship if the motion is separated into components.
	- We used the fact that projectile motion is a special case of two-dimensional motion that has zero acceleration in one dimension and constant, nonzero acceleration in the second dimension.
	- We analyzed various situations of an object in projectile motion keeping in mind that unless otherwise stated, projectile motion is modeled without including effects of air resistance.
