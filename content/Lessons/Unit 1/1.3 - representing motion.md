## notes
- ### lesson 1
	- [[finding velocity and acceleration from displacement to time graph]]
	- [[deriving velocity and displacement from constant and non-constant accelerations]]
	- Kinematic equations are only applicable when the [[acceleration]] of the object is **constant**.
	- [[displacement|Displacement]], [[velocity]], and [[acceleration]] vs. time graphs are connected to one another and should tell the same story.
	- When drawing graphs, start with placing dots for the initial and final conditions; then connect the dots with the appropriate line/curve.
- ### lesson 2
	- [[linearization|Linearization]] is used to transform non-linear data into a straight line, which allows for easier analysis and determination of relationships.
	- 
- ### lesson 3
	- [[finding relation between velocities with different accelerations and time]]
	- [[finding relation between displacements with different accelerations and time]]
	- ##### Steps:
		1. Determine which **kinematics equation** to apply.
		2. Derive an expression for the **original** scenario using only given variables and physical constants.
		3. Derive and expression for the **new** scenario using only given variables and physical constants.
		4. Combine the **original** and **new** equations.
- ### lesson 4
	- The slope of a [[displacement]]-time graph is [[velocity]].
		- The derivative of the [[displacement]] function with respect to time is the instantaneous [[velocity]].
	- The slope of a [[velocity]]-time graph is [[acceleration]].
		- The derivative of the [[velocity]] function with respect to time is the instantaneous [[acceleration]].
- ### lesson 5
	- The area of a [[velocity]]-time graph is [[displacement]].
		- The integral of the [[velocity]] function with respect to time is the [[displacement]].
	- The area of an [[acceleration]]-time graph is the change in velocity.
		- The integral of the [[acceleration]] function with respect to time is the change in [[velocity]].
