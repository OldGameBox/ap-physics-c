## notes
- ### lesson 1
	- [[center of mass|Center of mass]] is **the "average" location of all the matter in an object**. It is *the single point where*, for the purposes of physics, *the entire weight of the object seems to be concentrated*.
		- **How an object behaves is almost entirely dictated by what happens at this specific point.**
		- For example, when we *push an object*, its **behavior depends on where you apply the force relative to the** [[center of mass|CoM]].
			- If **we apply a force directly**, through the [[center of mass|CoM]], the object *will move in a straight line without spinning*.
			- If **we apply a force away** from the [[center of mass|CoM]], the object will *rotate around that center point while it moves*.
			- Even if an object is shaped weirdly, the [[center of mass|CoM]] will follow a smooth, predictable parabolic path, even if every other part of the object is flailing wildly. 
	- An object will **rotate** about its [[center of mass]].
		- For example, imagine a baseball bat thrown into air at angle. The bat will rotate around its [[center of mass|CoM]].
		- ![[Pasted image 20260224153658.png]]
	- An object will **balance** if there's support for its [[center of mass|CoM]].
	- The **location** of an object's [[center of mass|CoM]] **can change** if the object's *mass distribution changes*.
	- **Locating [[center of mass]] by inspection**
		- **Symmetrical objects of uniform density:**
			- If the object is symmetrical, its [[center of mass|CoM]] is along a line of symmetry.
				- ![[Pasted image 20260224154208.png]]
			- The [[center of mass|CoM]] is a point in space; it **doesn't need to be directly on the object**.
				- ![[Pasted image 20260224154341.png]]
	- **Locating [[center of mass]] by experiment**
		- **Using Gravity**
			- We are using the fact that **gravity will always act on an object at its** [[center of mass]].
				- ![[20260224-2049-00.5910886.mp4]]
	- **Locating [[center of mass]] mathematically**
		- $x$-axis
			- $$x_{cm}=\frac{{\sum m_{i}x_{i}}}{\sum m_{i}}$$
