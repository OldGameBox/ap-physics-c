## assignment
- ### part 1
	- $\vec{V} = 225\,m$ at $50^\circ$ west of north $=-172\hat{i}+145\hat{j}$ .
	- $\vec{U} = 130\,m$ at $32^\circ$ west of north $=-110\hat{i}+-69\hat{j}$ .
	- Find $\vec{V}+\vec{U}$ and $\vec{V}-\vec{U}$
- ### part 2
	- $\vec{v_{1}}=v_{1i}\hat{i}+v_{1j}\hat{j}$
	- $\vec{v_{2}}=v_{2i}\hat{i}+v_{2k}\hat{k}$
	- Find $\vec{v_{1}}+\vec{v_{2}}$

## solution
- ### part 1
	- In such simple problem, since we are already *given components of each axis* of each [[vector]], we simply **add/subtract each component of each corresponding axes**.
	- $$\begin{gather}\vec{V}+\vec{U}=(-172+-110)\hat{i}+(145+-69)\hat{j} \\ \vec{V}+\vec{U}=-282\hat{i}+76\hat{j} \\ \\ \vec{V}-\vec{U}=(-172--110)\hat{i}+(145--69)\hat{j} \\ \vec{V}-\vec{U}=-62\hat{i}+214\hat{j}\end{gather}$$
- ### part 2
	- In this *part 2* we can notice that $\vec{v_{1}}$ and $\vec{v_{2}}$ have a **common first axis** - $\hat{i}$ axis, but **different second axes** - $\hat{j}$ and $\hat{k}$ axes.
	- Such situation might look unconventional, however, it is totally possible and solvable.
	- If a *vector* **doesn't explicitly mention an axis**, its **coordinate for that axis is simply 0**.
	- Thus, all we have to do is to add $0\hat{k}$ to $\vec{v_{1}}$ and $0\hat{j}$ to $\vec{v_{2}}$.
	- $$\begin{gather} \vec{v_{1}}=v_{1i}\hat{i}+v_{1j}\hat{j}+0\hat{k} \\ \vec{v_{2}}=v_{2i}\hat{i}+0\hat{j}+v_{2k}\hat{k} \end{gather}$$
	- So, our final result will be:
	- $$\begin{gather} \vec{v_{1}} + \vec{v_{2}} = (v_{1i}+v_{2i})\hat{i}+v_{1j}\hat{j}+v_{2k}\hat{k} \end{gather}$$
